# complete E11-core Wang scanning and saturated-domain transversality

> Historical research record. Read with the [correction guide](../../../corrections.md); recorded conclusions are not new verification receipts.

This note records corrections and new execution evidence for the finite-field fixed-A route toward a possible rank-19 E11 core, which would lift to a rank-≤22 full algorithm over \(\mathbb F_2\).

## Corrections to analysis interpretation

1. [core_e11_saturation_v2.py](../../structural_obstruction/code/core_e11_saturation_v2.py) was a useful partial separator, but its `wang_violations` field scanned only subspaces generated by at most five selected points. It did **not** scan all 417,199 quotient subspaces. Complete Wang admissibility for a core support must be checked against the full core table.

2. The analysis core CP-SAT variables were Boolean projective points. This is a distinct-A construction ansatz. A rank-19 core tensor decomposition may repeat projected A-directions unless a separate multiplicity argument excludes them; a Boolean infeasibility result would not by itself exclude all rank-19 cores.

3. The valid cn122 E11-core positive control is obtained by quotienting all 23 full A-factors, discarding only terms whose projected A-factor is zero, and retaining the 21 surviving rank-one terms with their multiplicities and B/C factors. It has 18 distinct projected A-directions and multiplicity histogram `{1:15, 2:3}`.

4. Saturation gives more than nonzero term domains and linear tensor membership. If a contraction \(q\) is saturated, \(|I_q|=\operatorname{rank}L(q)\), then the participating \(b_s\) must be linearly independent and the participating \(c_s\) must be linearly independent. For the globally intersected domains \(B_s,C_s\), Rado's theorem gives the necessary conditions
\[
\dim\sum_{s\in J} B_s \ge |J|,\qquad
\dim\sum_{s\in J} C_s \ge |J|
\]
for every subset \(J\subseteq I_q\). These are independent-transversal tests for the shared factors.

## Positive control

The genuine cn122 E11-core rank-21 restriction verifies exactly and passes all corrected tests:

- 21 surviving terms, 18 distinct projected A-directions, multiplicity histogram `{1:15, 2:3}`.
- Complete core Wang scan at \(s=21\): 0 violations across all 417,199 rows.
- Saturated-domain test: 9 saturated contractions, no undersaturated contractions, no zero-domain terms, linear membership passes with 945 domain variables, coefficient rank 396, affine dimension 549.
- Actual cn122 B/C factors all lie in the computed domains.
- Independent-transversal test: 0 failures.

This validates the quotient convention and the new transversality test on a known decomposition.

## Re-evaluation of analysis core candidates

The partial-scan counts were optimistic. Complete counts are larger and the strongest-looking analysis saturation-clean support is also rejected by collective independence.

Selected examples from `analysis.json`:

- analysis round 1: partial 226 Wang violations becomes 367 complete violations. It has zero zero-domain terms but the linear membership test fails. The transversality test reports 37 failures; e.g. for \(q=34\), four C-domains for terms `[1,2,8,10]` have sum dimension 3, not 4.
- analysis round 5: partial 97 Wang violations becomes 214 complete violations. It has no zero-domain terms and linear membership passes (affine dimension 315), but the independent-transversal test reports 3 failures. Example: for saturated \(q=107\), the B-domains of terms `[2,6,7,10,12,14,18]` have sum dimension 6 although 7 independent B-factors are needed; the corresponding A-points are `[5,28,36,130,146,195,255]`. There is also a C-side failure for the same contraction.
- analysis round 7: partial 40 violations becomes 81 complete violations; it still has zero-domain obstructions.

Thus no analysis core support is close to a rank-19 construction under complete Wang checking. The round-5 support remains useful because it exposed a nontrivial collective-independence obstruction not detected by zero-domain or linear membership alone.

## Re-evaluation of the analysis combined rep2 support

The combined adjacent-pair support from [combined_sep_all5.json](../../quotient_cores/results/dual_ruling/combined_sep_all5.json) still passes zero-domain and linear membership tests, but fails the new transversality test:

- 20 saturated contractions, zero zero-domain terms, linear membership passes with affine dimension 549.
- 4 independent-transversal failures. A representative witness: for saturated \(q=77\), the C-domains of terms `[0,2,4,9]` have dimensions `[3,3,3,3]` but span only a 3-dimensional sum, so four independent C-factors cannot be chosen. The corresponding A-points are `[1,4,8,56]`.

This means the previously strongest rep2 support is not a fixed-A decomposition candidate even before complete Wang admissibility or a quadratic rank-one solve.

## Corrected distinct-point E11 core pilot

`core_e11_saturation_v3_complete.py` keeps the distinct-point Boolean ansatz but uses all core rows when evaluating each support and includes contraction lower bounds. In a 6-round pilot with all 1D/2D rows initially, complete scans after each solve, and exact-support exclusions for fixed-A failures:

- Round 0: 409 complete Wang violations, no zero-domain terms, linear pass, 18 transversal failures.
- Round 4: best support in this pilot: 29 complete Wang violations, no zero-domain terms, linear pass, affine dimension 342, but 4 transversality failures. Support: `[1,2,3,4,8,9,16,18,32,72,96,144,146,160,224,227,243,252,255]`.
- Round 5: 75 complete Wang violations and zero-domain obstructions.

The v3 pilot is a corrected construction-search instrument, but it is still a distinct-A search and does not prove lower bounds. A support must pass complete Wang, zero-domain, linear membership, and transversality before a B/C rank-one completion attempt is meaningful.

## Current scientific effect

The E11 core route remains active because a rank-19 core decomposition would still yield a rank-≤22 full algorithm over \(\mathbb F_2\). The corrected evidence, however, shows that the prior low partial-violation counts were not reliable indicators of Wang admissibility. The new transversality test is a real strengthening of shared-factor compatibility: it rejects both a saturation-clean core support and the strongest combined rep2 support while passing the genuine cn122 core positive control.

The next useful computational development is to encode transversality witnesses as reified support cuts: if a listed subset of A-points is present and the contractions whose saturation forced their domains remain saturated, then at least one of those saturation assumptions must be broken. Such cuts should be applied before expensive rank-one solving, with exact provenance for the saturated contractions and domain-sum certificate.
