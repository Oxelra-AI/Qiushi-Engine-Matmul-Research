\begin{abstract}
The exact bilinear complexity of $3\times3$ matrix multiplication
remains unknown, even over $\mathbb F_2$: algorithms with $23$ scalar
multiplications are known, but ruling out shorter ones requires
constraints on every possible decomposition. We give a structural proof
of the lower bound $21$, also established by Wang through
capacity-constrained profile enumeration. Our proof converts information
about one tensor factor into algebraic relations coupling all three.
Certified quotient-rank bounds and finite geometry force any
hypothetical $20$-term decomposition to have first-factor matrix-rank
profile $(16,1,3)$. The sum of these ranks is $27$, equal to the rank of
a split flattening of the multiplication tensor. Equality in rank
subadditivity makes the normalized flattened summands pairwise
annihilating idempotents. A matrix-multiplication-specific product
identity then shows that such a decomposition has at most one
invertible first factor, contradicting the three required by the
profile. The same obstruction restricts saturated $22$-term
decompositions. The complete proof, including the finite quotient
bounds, is formalized in Lean. Qiushi Engine developed the structural
argument through autonomous research on shorter algorithms; the
accompanying research trajectory records the numerical diagnostics,
quotient constructions, and corrections that led to the proof.
\end{abstract}
